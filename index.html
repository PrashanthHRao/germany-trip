⚠️ Safety Briefing

  • Munich is Germany's safest major city (top of police stats 7 years running) and rated LOW-threat by US OSAC. The right choice for a family with a baby.
  • Avoid Berlin — multiple protests on Nov 14, 20, 25 including youth "Gen Z regelt" demos with reported violence. MEDIUM-threat location.
  • Nov 20 (Thursday): "Gen Z regelt" protests in Munich city center. Day 1 is Sat Nov 22 — well clear of protest dates.
  • Germany national terror level = "high" (upgraded July 2026) — this is standard national posture. Christmas markets have enhanced security with visible police presence. No specific threats to Munich.
  • Petty crime: Watch for pickpockets at Christmas markets and Hauptbahnhof. Keep passports/baby documents in hotel safe.
  • Stroller-friendly: Munich has wide sidewalks, smooth paving, lifts at stations. Most attractions are accessible.

Medieval half-timbered buildings, the Imperial Castle, and the fortified city walls — Nuremberg's Altstadt is one of Germany's most beautifully preserved old towns. The cobblestone streets are manageable with a stroller on the main routes. Start at the castle and work down toward Hauptmarkt.

Christkindlesmarkt, Nuremberg ★ Must-See
Germany's most famous Christmas market — over 160 wooden Buden (booths) on Hauptmarkt. This is THE authentic German Christmas experience: Nürnberger Bratwurst (skip), Lebkuchen gingerbread (great vegan options!), roasted almonds, and the iconic Nürnberger Rostbratwurst market flag. The Christkind (Christ child) greets visitors in traditional costume. Hot apple juice and chai for non-drinkers. Stroller-friendly with wide market aisles.

💇 Baby on flights: Infant (under 2) flies on lap for ~15% of adult fare with own ticket, or ~€10 on lap without seat. Bring birth certificate or passport. Both airlines offer bassinets on long-haul but this is short-haul so request baby amenities at booking.

💡 Tips

  • Weather: November Munich is 2–8°C, likely rain. Pack waterproof layers, warm hats for baby and adults, waterproof stroller cover
  • Christmas markets close early: Most close by 20:00-21:00 in November — plan indoor activities for evenings
  • Book ahead: Gratitude (birthday dinner), Nymphenburg gondola, and ICE train to Nuremberg
  • Currency: Euros (€). Most places accept cards, but keep €50-100 cash for markets and small purchases
  • Passports: Carry them (Irish citizens need valid passport for Germany). Store baby's passport copy digitally
  • EU Health Insurance Card: Get one for the baby before traveling — free at any Irish pharmacy
  • Best photo light: December skies get dark by 16:00 — do outdoor sights before 15:00, Christmas markets after dark for the magic
  • Zugspitze (Top of Germany): Germany's highest peak (2,962m) is ~1.5h by train from Munich. Unfortunately all cable cars are closed Nov 23-26 for autumn maintenance. The Partnachklamm Gorge in Garmisch-Partenkirchen is open (€10, 8-18) — dramatic rock walls and rushing river.
  • Stroller storage: Trains have dedicated spaces, S-Bahn has luggage areas. Consider a lightweight umbrella stroller
